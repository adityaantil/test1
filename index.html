A chip never tells you its secret. But it hums while it works.

A chip gives away clues through its power use, its timing and its memory. HardSec 2026 teaches you to read those clues, find weaknesses and show how hardware can be made safer. You do not need to be an expert. You only need to be curious.

DAY 1

Learn to read the leak

  • Memory vulnerabilities and real attack surfaces
  • Side-channel leakage, from first principles to correlation
  • Fault injection: how one glitch becomes a bypass
  • Guided practice that prepares you for every Day 2 track
DAY 2

Break the cipher

  • A hackathon in three phases: discover, engineer and invent
  • Each phase is a track with its own challenge and tools
  • On-site or remote teams compete on the same leaderboard
  • Prizes for the top teams and certificates for all

Three phases. Three tracks.

First find the weakness, then build the defense, and finally bring an idea of your own.

  1. Phase 1 · Track A

    Discover the vulnerability

    Identify an access-control failure, data corruption, predictable access pattern, or information leakage in a provided memory model. Use Python or C to analyze access logs and demonstrate the weakness.

    • Python
    • C
  2. Phase 2 · Track B

    Engineer the defense

    Implement a secure memory wrapper, access monitor, address checker, integrity mechanism, or another protection technique using Verilog/SystemVerilog and Vivado.

    • Verilog / SystemVerilog
    • Vivado
  3. Phase 3 · Track C

    Invent your own solution

    Propose a different attack or defense, compare alternative architectures, or build an automated vulnerability detector. Novel approaches are welcome if the team can explain and validate the result.

    • Your choice of tools

Teams are judged on technical depth, a working demonstration, a clear explanation and validation of the result. Day 1 prepares you for every track, so no expert knowledge is needed.

Frequently asked questions

I am in first year and only know basic coding. Can I join?

Yes. Basic Python, C or Verilog (any one) is enough. Day 1 teaches the security ideas from the ground up.

What do I need to bring?

Only a laptop (Windows or Linux). Setup details will be shared after registration.

What are the three phases?

Track A: discover a vulnerability in a provided memory model. Track B: engineer a defense in Verilog/SystemVerilog. Track C: invent your own attack, defense or detector. Day 1 prepares you for all three.

Can I take part remotely?

Yes. Remote teams compete on the same leaderboard and for the same prizes as on-site teams.

Can I take part solo or in a team?

Both are fine. You can take part solo or in a team of 2 to 4 members.

How are teams judged?

On technical depth, a working demonstration, how clearly you explain it, and whether you can validate your result.

Is it free?

Entry is free for IEEE/CASS student members. Everyone else pays a registration fee of ₹549.

Do I get a certificate?

Yes. Every participant gets a certificate, and the top teams share prizes of ₹20,000+.

When does registration open?

Soon. The event dates are tentative until registration opens. Email us and we will let you know as soon as it does.
